Rent vs Buy UK

Why this is harder than it looks

The rent vs buy comparison is usually reduced to a monthly payment — which misses most of the picture. Buying involves upfront costs (SDLT, legal fees, survey), ongoing costs renters don't face (maintenance, insurance), and the opportunity cost of the deposit. The honest answer: buying wins financially over 7+ years in most UK markets.

What each option actually costs

Buying costs

  • Monthly mortgage payment
  • SDLT (upfront, non-recoverable)
  • Conveyancing and survey
  • Maintenance (~1% value/yr)
  • Buildings insurance
  • Ground rent / service charge
  • Foregone return on deposit

Renting costs

  • Monthly rent
  • Contents insurance
  • No equity accumulated
  • Exposure to rent increases
  • Deposit tied up (but investable)
  • No capital gain
  • Flexibility to relocate

When renting genuinely makes sense

Renting is the right financial choice when you expect to move within 5 years, when house price growth in your target area is low, when your deposit generates better returns invested elsewhere, or when your financial situation is likely to change significantly.

Bottom line

In most UK markets, buying is financially superior over 7+ years. SDLT drag and transaction costs mean buying is wrong for short stays. The opportunity cost of the deposit is real but typically outweighed by leveraged equity growth over time. The exact break-even depends on your rent, mortgage rate and price growth.

Frequently asked questions

Is renting throwing money away?
No — rent pays for housing services, exactly as mortgage interest does (interest does not build equity). The difference is that capital repayments build equity. The "throwing money away" framing oversimplifies the comparison.
What house price growth should I assume?
UK house prices have grown approximately 3–4% annually on a long-run average with significant regional variation. Model a range of 0–5% to understand sensitivity — the break-even year is highly sensitive to this assumption.
Should I include mortgage interest as a buying cost?
Yes — only the capital repayment portion builds equity. The interest portion is a cost comparable to rent. In the early years of a repayment mortgage, the majority of each payment is interest.
Does the BTL surcharge affect the rent vs buy decision?
